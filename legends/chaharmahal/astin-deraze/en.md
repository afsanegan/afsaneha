Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari** (encircling the historical domains of **کوهرنگ / زردکوه**), the legendary lore of **Astin Deraze** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Astin Deraze** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Yalda Rasekhi According to the beliefs of the people of Hafshejan village (Located in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari province in the southwestern part of Iran), there was a creature named ‘Astin Deraze', which translates to ‘the one with long sleeves’. Astin Deraze is a creature similar to a black spirit. Its appearance is not fully visible. This creature has very long sleeves and uses them to hide the children it kidnaps and takes with itself. Parents use this creature to scare children. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Astin Deraze** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Astin Deraze** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
