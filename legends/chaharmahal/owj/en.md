Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari** (encircling the historical domains of **کوهرنگ / زردکوه**), the legendary lore of **Owj** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Owj** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour There's a creature in Bakhtiari (western part of Iran) mythology named [Owj]. He is so tall that he can fish with his long hands and grill it using Sun's heat. According to the myths, he lived in 'Bazoft valley' (located in the western part of Iran) with his mother.They say that once 'Owj' felt extreme pain in his legs and stretched them in the 'Bazoft' river. He asked his mother to spread ointment on his feet, but it didn't work on his pain. After a while, his legs started to dry. They got dried and made a natural stone bridge on the river. He then lost his life. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Owj** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Owj** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
