Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad** (encircling the historical domains of **یاسوج / دنا**), the legendary lore of **Eem-Oui** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Eem-Oui** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami According to some tales told by the people of Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad Province (southwestern Iran), there used to be a creature named 'Eem-oui'. Eem-oui was an aquatic creature. It lived in the water and looked like a human but was made out of water. It is said that this creature was the reason behind people drowning in the rivers and pools. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Eem-Oui** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Eem-Oui** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
