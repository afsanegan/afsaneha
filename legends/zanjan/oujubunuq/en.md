Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Zanjan** (encircling the historical domains of **زنجان**), the legendary lore of **Oujubunuq** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Oujubunuq** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami Oujubunugh is the name of a giant in Zanjani folk tales (Central Iran). It is said that Oujubinugh was so big that could not find clothing to cover himself fully. He would roam about in the countryside, pillaging and stealing from villages. People of one certain village gathered to find a solution to the danger that he posed. They thought that if they give the giant a gift, he’d leave them alone. So they gathered all the clothes in the village and sew him a robe and gifted it to him. Although the robe barely reached Oujubinugh’s knees, he was very pleased with such a gift and swore to them that he would not cause damage to their village anymore. So he did. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Oujubunuq** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Zanjan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Oujubunuq** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
