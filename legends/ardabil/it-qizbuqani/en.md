Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Ardabil** (encircling the historical domains of **سبلان**), the legendary lore of **It Giz buqani ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **It Giz buqani ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour In the beliefs of the people of Meshgin Shahr (A city in Ardebil, Northwestern Iran), there is a creature named [It Giz buqani]. It is a huge, black lizard. People believed that if the fire of the logs used in the over was out, this creature came out of it and attacked those who lived in that house. It took them to wastelands in a blink of an eye and left them there until they died of hunger and dehydration. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **It Giz buqani ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Ardabil to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **It Giz buqani ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
