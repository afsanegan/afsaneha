Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Ardabil** (encircling the historical domains of **سبلان**), the legendary lore of **Hodhod-e-Shane be sar ‌ ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Hodhod-e-Shane be sar ‌ ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Yalda Rasekhi Among people who live in Sooha village (a village in Ardabil province, located in the northwestern part of Iran), there is a folk tale about a bird named 'Hodhode Shaneh-be-Sar'. This tale is about a newlywed bride. One day when she was combing her hair, her father-in-law came in and saw her without a hijab. Embarrassed, she asked God to turn her into a bird. She wanted to leave the house quietly. The bride turned into a hodhod (hoopoe) and the comb in her hand changed into the crown of feathers on the hoopoe's head. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Hodhod-e-Shane be sar ‌ ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Ardabil to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Hodhod-e-Shane be sar ‌ ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
