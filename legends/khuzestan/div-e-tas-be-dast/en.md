Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Khuzestan** (encircling the historical domains of **خوزستان (شوش باستان و انشان)**), the legendary lore of **Div-e Tas be dast ‌ ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Div-e Tas be dast ‌ ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *Div*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour According to the people living in Khorasan, there's a Div (demon) named 'Div-e Tas Be Dast', which translates to 'the demon with a bowl in its hand'. This Div looks like a human. The upper half of its body is hairless and full of white spots and the lower half of its body is full of hair.Div-e Tas Be Dast has a bowl made of copper in its hand and waits in the fields or on the roads to mountains and asks passersby for water. If they don't help, he will hit them in the head with his bowl and the person would go unconscious. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Div-e Tas be dast ‌ ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Khuzestan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Div-e Tas be dast ‌ ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
