Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Khuzestan** (encircling the historical domains of **شوش / شوشتر**), the legendary lore of **Hasan Rishi** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Hasan Rishi** within the mythological taxonomy of *Jin*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour Hasan Rishi is the name given to a Jin, popular in the folk culture of south western Iranians. It was said that Hasan Rishi was able to shape shift into a goose, or a goat, though often it would appear as a human and demand to get a piggyback ride from people. Translation: Zeinab Hashemi …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Hasan Rishi** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Khuzestan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Hasan Rishi** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
