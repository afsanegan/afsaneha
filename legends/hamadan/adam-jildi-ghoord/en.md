Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hamadan** (encircling the historical domains of **همدان / الوند**), the legendary lore of **Adam Jildi Ghoord** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Adam Jildi Ghoord** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Yalda Rasekhi In the belief of Turkish people living in Hamedan, there is a humanoid wolf which is called, Adam Jildi Ghoord. It is said that this creature came to the village and hunted small children and sheep. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Adam Jildi Ghoord** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hamadan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Adam Jildi Ghoord** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
