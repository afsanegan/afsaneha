Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Kermanshah** (encircling the historical domains of **کرمانشاه / بیستون**), the legendary lore of **Kulesh** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Kulesh** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami In the Ilam and western parts of Kermanshah (western Iran), there have been tales about a dangerous and terrifying creature that dwells in the Zagros mountains. The creature has animalistic features and looks like a cross between a hyena and a wolf. It's called [Kulish]. According to the tales from Abdanan city in Ilam, this creature eats humans. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Kulesh** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Kermanshah to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Kulesh** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
