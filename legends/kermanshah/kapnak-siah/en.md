Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Kermanshah** (encircling the historical domains of **کرمانشاه / بیستون**), the legendary lore of **Kapnak Siah** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Kapnak Siah** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami Kapnak Siah, according to the people of 'Dousvand' clan in Kermanshah (western part of Iran), is the name of the clan's totem. This totem can appear in many forms among the people of the clan but the way most of them have described him as is a very tall man in a long sheppard's jacket that's made of felt. People of the clan believe in this totem and for showing gratitude and protecting it, they offer oblations. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Kapnak Siah** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Kermanshah to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Kapnak Siah** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
