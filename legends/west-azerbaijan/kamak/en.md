Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **West Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **ارومیه**), the legendary lore of **KAMAK** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **KAMAK** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour Kamak is a huge mythical bird in the “Sad Bandehshan” story, who would bring starvation, drought, and death to the lands and all the living until a mythical hero, Garshasb, destroys it.Kamak would stop the rain with its humongous wings and pour it all into the sea so no water could land on the dry lands.During seven rainy days and nights, Garshasb throws arrows to the sky until Kamak is finally injured and falls to the ground.Garshasb and Kamak’s battle is written in the Pahlavi Texts with more details.There is a similar story in the Chinese tradition about “Shin Yi” who fought a similar creature and saved everyone. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **KAMAK** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of West Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **KAMAK** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
