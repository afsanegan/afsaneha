Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **West Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **ارومیه**), the legendary lore of **Three Legged Donkey ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Three Legged Donkey ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Khar-e-Sepa|Khar Se pa Illustration by mo.rasoulipour The “Three-Legged Donkey” is referenced in the ancient Iranian cosmological text, the Bundaheshn. This gigantic mythical creature’s size and characteristics are incomprehensible to us mortals; Khar-e-Sepa had three legs, six eyes, nine mouths, two ears, and a shining golden horn that branched out into smaller and smaller horns. This offshoot of horns served to destroy vile and corrupt creatures. Each of the donkey’s nine mouths could devour mountains. The titan lived by the sea and its movements provoked colossal floods. It is believed that Khar-e-Sepā helped Tishtra, the goddess of fertility and rain, to distribute water across the earth. When the Three-Legged Donkey let out a cry, it impregnated all holy marine creatures. When it urinated, Khar-e-Sepā purified the mythical ocean of Frakhvkart. Unicorn"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Three Legged Donkey ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of West Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Three Legged Donkey ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
