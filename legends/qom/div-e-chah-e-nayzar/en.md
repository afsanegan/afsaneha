In the scorched salt marshes and desolate salt basins of southwestern Qom, near Salafchegan and the historical desert hamlet of *Nayzar*, where encrusted salt flats meet arid reed brakes, seasoned camel-drivers historically shunned a deadly mythological entity known as the *Div of the Salt Well*. This subterranean demon is an incarnation of the arid salt flats, his skin encrusted with razor-sharp salt crystals and his jagged teeth hewn from flint, dwelling within abandoned saline wells and bottomless mud flats.

Regional folk tradition recounts that the Div of the Salt Well is a consummate weaver of midday mirages. Refracting blinding solar glare through salt crusts, the demon projects optical illusions of magnificent caravanserais boasting shaded colonnades, cool fountains, and swaying palms in the horizon. Thirst-maddened travelers and camel caravans veer off the established trail toward the phantom oasis. As they plunge into the heart of the mirage, the brittle salt crust gives way beneath the camels' hooves, and the laughing titan pulls them down into subterranean saline chasms, petrifying flesh and bone into salt statues.

> "In the sands of Nayzar, cast not your hopes upon the false shimmering sea; that brilliance is the bared teeth of the Salt Well Div gleaming under the sun to pickle the unwary." — Adage of the old caravaneers of Qom.

Scholar Seyyed Abolghasem Enjavi Shirazi in *Persian Tales* and Mohammad-Taqi Beyk Qomi in *Folk Beliefs of Qom* explain that this narrative served as an indispensable cautionary compass against treacherous desert quicksands and disorienting mirages across the Lake Howz-e Soltan basin. Caravans affixed bronze bells to their lead camels and followed trained hounds, knowing animals could distinguish true water from fatal salt mirages. The silent mudbrick ruins and sparkling flats of Nayzar at sunset remain an enduring reminder of human vigilance amidst the perils of the desert.
