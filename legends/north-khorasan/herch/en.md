Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **North Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **بجنورد**), the legendary lore of **HERCH** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **HERCH** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour Herch, Known in local northeast (Khorasani) Iranian folklore, is a monkey-like creature that is small in stature, hairy and shiny-eyed. It was believed to grapple sheep in the evenings and exhaust them. This creature was known to be one of the main reasons for sheep or cattle to lose weight. It is said that the local sheep herders were able to capture one such creature by applying a sticky substance (Ghareghorut) to the sheep so it wasn’t able to escape." …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **HERCH** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of North Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **HERCH** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
