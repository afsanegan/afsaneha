Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Isfahan** (encircling the historical domains of **اصفهان / کاشان**), the legendary lore of **Mool** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Mool** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Mul | Muul | Mūl Illustration by Farima Keshavarz In the belief of the Isfahani people (central Iran), there is a creature called Mool. He is a creature with the body of a mouse or a lizard with a head similar to a human baby. People believe that sometimes some women give birth to a Mool instead of a normal baby. The mole eats the fetus in the womb of a mother and replaces it in the mother's body. The child is born earlier than the birth time, and as soon as it is born and leaves the mother's body, it reaches the wall and climbs over it. People believe that this creature should be killed and buried quickly. This narrative creates fear in pregnant women and their families. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Mool** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Isfahan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Mool** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
