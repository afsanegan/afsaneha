Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Kerman** (encircling the historical domains of **کرمان / بم**), the legendary lore of **MORGH-E-SHO** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **MORGH-E-SHO** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour In the folklore of the people of southern Kerman, "Morgh Shu" is a golden-colored bird capable of changing its size. This ominous bird usually appears at night and, by drinking the blood of infants, causes their death. According to the belief of the people in this region, before Morgh Shu attacks an infant at night, it places its ten magical tokens in the water reserve or in the parents' slippers to put them into a deep sleep. Morgh Shu is believed to be able to enter the infants' sleeping quarters even through small openings. It has also been believed that killing this malevolent bird is not auspicious and can lead to dire consequences for the perpetrators. People believe that by swearing to the sacred, Morgh Shu will not harm their infants. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **MORGH-E-SHO** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Kerman to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **MORGH-E-SHO** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
