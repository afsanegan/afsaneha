Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Kerman** (encircling the historical domains of **کرمان / بم**), the legendary lore of **Kheir-o-shar** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Kheir-o-shar** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami There is a creature named 'Kheir-o-Shar' (meaning 'good and evil'). This creature appears in the twilight as a man. If he encounters a human and the human notices him, he asks about the time. When the human tells him the time, he grows so tall that he reaches the skies and then he disappears. Whoever sees him, faints out of fear. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Kheir-o-shar** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Kerman to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Kheir-o-shar** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
