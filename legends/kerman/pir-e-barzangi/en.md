Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Kerman** (encircling the historical domains of **کرمان / بم**), the legendary lore of **Pir-e-Barzangi** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Pir-e-Barzangi** within the mythological taxonomy of *Div*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Yalda Rasekhi 'Pir Barzangi' or 'Pirozangi', according to the people of Torbat Heidarieh (southeastern Iran), is the name of a Div (demon) that looks like a human. This div has appeared in two different shapes. In some tales, he looks exactly like a human. He lives among humans and gets everything he wants by deceiving people. If anyone finds out about Pir Barzangi's secrets, he will cut their tongue off.In other tales, Pir Barzani is the name of a mother div that has many children. It lives inside wells, caves, or cottages inside gardens. Pir Barzangi has long and wrinkly breasts, and it puts them on its shoulders. It also uses magic. Pir Barzangi, unlike its appearance, helps fictional heroes not to get eaten by his sons. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam None"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Pir-e-Barzangi** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Kerman to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Pir-e-Barzangi** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
