Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **VIZARSH DIV** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **VIZARSH DIV** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami Vizarsh, is the name given to a Deev in Zoroastrian belief. It is said that, after death, the sinner's soul lingers on earth next to the body for three days. During this period, Vizarsh torments the guilty soul for its evil deeds. Finally, Vizarsh takes the weeping soul wrathfully to the Chinvat bridge (bridge of judgment) and then to hell on the fourth day. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **VIZARSH DIV** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **VIZARSH DIV** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
