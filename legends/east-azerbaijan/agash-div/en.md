Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **AGASH DIV** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **AGASH DIV** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour "Agash, the Deev, is named in ancient Iranian texts including, the Bundaheshn, and his name is associated with 'evil eye' and bad thoughts about others. Agash Deev makes people forget good thoughts and instead dwell on the bad. Not much more is known about this Deev. This name is found in Middle Persian as ağaşay and in Avestan -ağašay and it means having an evil eye." … Chīstā, Volume 20, Issues 194-200"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **AGASH DIV** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **AGASH DIV** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
