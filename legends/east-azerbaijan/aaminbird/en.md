Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **The Aamin Bird** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **The Aamin Bird** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Morgh-e-Aamin|The Amen Bird Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour In the folklore and culture of Iranian people, the Awmeen Bird is the name of an angel who is always flying and constantly says Awmeen (Amen). It is known in popular belief that if the Awmeen Bird is in flight during a prayer or a curse, it will cause the curse or prayer to be answered. Awmeen is also the name of the star "Sirius, “ believed to answer prayers when it rises. Awmeen Bird is also mentioned several times in modern poetry by Nima Youshij. … …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **The Aamin Bird** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **The Aamin Bird** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
