Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **Hvar Khshaita** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Hvar Khshaita** within the mythological taxonomy of *Deity*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour Hvare-khshaeta is the Avestan language name of the Zoroastrian Divinity of the "Radiant Sun."The word ‘hvar’ derives from Indo-Iranian *súHar "sun", from which the Vedic Sanskrit name of deity Surya in Hindu culture is also drived from. In Middle Persian, Hvare-khshaeta, changed to Khwarshēd, wich today is know as Khorshīd meaning the Sun.The Sixth Yasht of Zoroastrian Avesta is in praise of this Hvare-khshaeta. Ancient Iranians celebrated the 11th day of each month in honor of this deity."

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Hvar Khshaita** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Hvar Khshaita** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
