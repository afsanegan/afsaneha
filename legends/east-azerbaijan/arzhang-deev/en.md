Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **ARZHANG DEEV** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **ARZHANG DEEV** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami "Arzhang-e Deev" was one of the leaders of the Deevs (demons) of Mazandaran, whose description we can read from the extensive ""Seven Labors of Rostam"" in the Shahnameh. Following Kavous's (the King of Iran) attack on Mazandaran, Iranian soldiers were captured and blinded by the White Demon. The White Deev held them captive in Mount Espand, seizing their treasures (the Shah's treasure) and appointing Arzhang Deev as their guardian. In the sixth labor, when Rostam learns of Arzhang Deev's whereabouts, he cries out a thunderous call from outside the demon's tent, causing the earth and sky to tremble, compelling Arzhang Deev to emerge from his tent. Upon seeing him, Rostam swiftly charges towards Arzhang Deev on horseback, severing his head from his body, and flings the head towards the assembly of Deevs. Witnessing Arzhang's demise, the onlookers are terrified and attempt to flee, but Rostam draws his sword, slaying them." …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **ARZHANG DEEV** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **ARZHANG DEEV** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
