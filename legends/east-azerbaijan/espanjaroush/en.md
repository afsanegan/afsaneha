Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **Espanjaroush DIV** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Espanjaroush DIV** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami According to Mazdasena myths, he is a deity who resides in the clouds and prevents the gods from raining, to put it simply, his job is to prevent rain. The demon Spongerosh is the helper of a demon named Apposh, who is destroyed by "Vazasht" or lightning, after its destruction, rain starts to fall. Its narration is that, when Tishtar (the god of rain) starts to rain, she finds Spongerosh and Aposh in front of her; The fire moves its mace and heats the drops of water in the sky clouds; As a result of the impact of that hit, the Spanjarosh growls and roars, this roar is the one heard from the thunder before the rain. In some narrations, the reason for the saltiness of the seawater is known to be the battle between demon Aposh and Spanjarosh with Tishtar, who fought over the rain and as a result, there was thunder and lightning and a salty rain that poured its water into the seas and oceans. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Espanjaroush DIV** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Espanjaroush DIV** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
