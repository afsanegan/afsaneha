Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **KHURTAN KHOLEE** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **KHURTAN KHOLEE** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz "In the belief of the people of Rudqat region near Tabriz, in northwestern Iran, there is a belief in the existence of a powerful genie named "Khortan-Kholee". It is said that a sorceress named "Shaman Memi" (Shaman Mahmoud) lived in this area next to the "Pir Bolaghi" spring. One day, the Khan's (Ruler's) son, who believed that he had a way to destroy their realm's enemies, asked the shamans to execute a curse so he could take his father's place as the Khan (ruler). The Shaman Memi requests lots of money from the Khan's son and gets to work. While executing this complex curse, due to negligence, he mistakenly summons a powerful evil genie named "Khortan-Kholee" in one of the summoning stages. Khortan-Kholee emerges from the fire and takes the life of Shaman Memi. Khan's son and his men flee. The powerful genie then takes control of all genies in the region and brings chaos to the towns nearby by haunting vulnerable people, especially the young. The people of the villages band together to come up with a solution to this genie being on the loose, so with the help of an Ashik(Musician), knowing the genie is mesmerized by humans playing music, lure him to a valley named "Miruqaya" by a holy spring and entrap him there. Some believe that Khortan-Kholee was never trapped and is still on the loose. This local lore has caused people in this region to call anyone showing bad/erratic behaviors to call them Khortan-Kholee to this day." …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **KHURTAN KHOLEE** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **KHURTAN KHOLEE** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
