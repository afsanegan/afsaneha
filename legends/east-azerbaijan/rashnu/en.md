Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **Rashnu** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Rashnu** within the mythological taxonomy of *Deity*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour Is the Deity of Justice in Zoroastrianism religion. Together with Mithra and Sraosha, Rashnu is one of the three judges who pass judgment on the souls of people after death based on their good and bad deeds. He is described as having a golden scale through which an individual’s accumulated sins and virtues are measured. This balance “does not dip unjustly on any side: Not for the righteous ones, nor the sinful ones, not for lords nor other rulers. It does not alter so much as a single hair’s breadth and possesses no partiality."Ancient Iranians would celebrate the 18th day of each month in praise of Rashnu. Wearing new clothes and traveling were encouraged on this day. … https://iranicaonline.org/articles/rasn-yast"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Rashnu** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Rashnu** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
