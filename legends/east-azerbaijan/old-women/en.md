Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **OLD WOMEN IN IRANIAN Folklore** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **OLD WOMEN IN IRANIAN Folklore** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "In the beliefs of the Iranians, there are many creatures in the form of old women who are often evil and frightening. In many regions, there is a belief in a creature similar to "Āl." In the beliefs of the people of Tabriz (northwest of Iran), this creature is called "Hāli Naneh". She is a tall woman who wears a long necklace and harasses pregnant women. One day, Hāli Naneh was hanging from the ceiling of a tandir (oven) and was tormenting a pregnant woman. The woman grabbed her necklace, and a stone from Hāli Naneh’s necklace broke off. Hāli Naneh fell into the tandoor pit, and from that day onward, no one has seen her again. In Mahabad, West Azerbaijan, this creature is known as "Shahveh." To protect the mother after childbirth, they place a light, an open knife, and two sickles above her bed at night. In the beliefs of the people of Jahrom, Fars Province, there is also a creature called "Khaleh Ghoolak," who is known to kidnap infants. In many regions, creatures in the form of old women also appear to frighten and abduct mischievous children. One such creature is "Nane Har Dom," believed in by the people of Tehran. This ugly and terrifying old woman’s body is covered with the tails of various animals. If a child disobeys their mother, "Nane Har Dom" comes after them and asks which animal’s tail they want to be strangled with. Another old woman in the beliefs of the people of Fasa, Fars Province, is called "Nane Andar Ghorabā" (Mother of Exile). She is often imagined as an old woman with white hair, dressed in traditional Fasa women’s clothing. If children harassed adults, she would abduct them and take them to places far from home. Belief in this creature is widespread in Fars, but it is particularly well-known among the people of Fasa. A similar creature exists in the beliefs of the people of Shadegan, Khuzestan Province(southwest of Iran), known as "Omme Khazra va-Allīf" (Mother of Greenery). She is an old woman with long hair resembling palm branches that cover her body. "Omme Khazra va-Allīf" kidnaps children in palm groves. The people of Kabuderahang, Hamadan Province(west of Iran), also believe in a frightening creature called "Pīrezan Mārku," whose job is to scare children. However, not all old women in Iranian folklore are evil. One of the benevolent and angelic figures in the beliefs of the people of Lorestan(west of Iran) is "Kheyr Bar Kar." This angel visits herds and homes at night as a bird or an old woman and distributes sustenance among the people. Sometimes, people leave the lid of a cooking pot open so that "Kheyr Bar Kar" can eat from it, bringing them more blessings and sustenance. In previous posts, old women such as Om al-Masahi, Dada Kalako, Naneh Jon Jon, Daranj, Shuteh Zenayi, Barakh Barakh, Sakineh, Al Arvadi, Al Khato, Al Zangi, and others have been mentioned."

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **OLD WOMEN IN IRANIAN Folklore** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **OLD WOMEN IN IRANIAN Folklore** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
