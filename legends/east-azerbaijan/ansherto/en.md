Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **East Azerbaijan** (encircling the historical domains of **تبریز**), the legendary lore of **ĀNSHERTO** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **ĀNSHERTO** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami In the belief of the people of the Persian Gulf, there is a creature called "Ansherto". He is an agile amphibian with a small head, who speaks like a human and has a family. Anshertos have a fin-like appendage under their shoulder that they can use to help them swim like fish.It is a tradition that some people arrived with the fishing ships where the Ansherto lived and captured a few of them. When the sailors reached the middle of the sea, they were surprised to see that the Anshertos threw themselves overboard and ran away. Only one of them was not able to escape. She was a girl who was locked in the basement of the ship and tried to break through the bottom of the ship and escape, but the captain prevented her. The captain married that girl and had six children with her. But even after marriage, he tied her feet in the fear that she would run away. After some time, the children untied the mother's leg and fled into the sea. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **ĀNSHERTO** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of East Azerbaijan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **ĀNSHERTO** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
