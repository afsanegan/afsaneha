Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Fars** (encircling the historical domains of **شیراز / مرودشت**), the legendary lore of **XANNĀSE** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **XANNĀSE** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour According to the beliefs of the people living in Sedeh village (located in the southwestern part of Iran) and the local lore, there's a creature known as 'Xannaseh'. Despite its short height, Xannaseh moves quickly. In hot seasons, this creature hid in the deserts and palm groves, or behind the trees and fences and laughed evilly. Nowadays, the name, Xannaseh, is used to describe people who laugh all the time and for no reason.Translated by Sara Mohebbi … Oral history, Local Lore"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **XANNĀSE** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Fars to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **XANNĀSE** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
