Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Fars** (encircling the historical domains of **پارس کهن (تخت جمشید و استخر)**), the legendary lore of **MASHEE & MASHYANE** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **MASHEE & MASHYANE** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour Mashya and Mashyana were the first man and woman whose procreation gave rise to the human race based on Zoroastrian cosmology. Based on Bundahishn, When Ahriman (devil) invaded the world of AhuraMazda, Kiomars were killed by evil spirits. Kiomars fell into his left hand at his death, and sperm came out of him. The sperm of Kiomars remained on the earth for forty years. And in forty years, with the shape of a one-stemmed Rivas-plant, Mashya, and Mashyana grew up from the earth. They were so connected that it was unclear which was the male and which was the female. They learned farming, lighting fire, and milking goats from angels. They did not have sex for fifty years, and sexual desire awoke in them after this time. From them was born in nine months a pair, and owing to tenderness for offspring the mother devoured one, and the father one. Ahura Mazda took this tenderness away, so that one may nourish a child. After that, seven pairs of male and female offspring emerged from them, who are believed to be the human race's ancestors. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **MASHEE & MASHYANE** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Fars to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **MASHEE & MASHYANE** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
