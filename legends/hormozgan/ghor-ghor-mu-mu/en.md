Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Ghor-Ghor-Momo** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Ghor-Ghor-Momo** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz Among the people of Qazvin (central Iran), there is a tale about a creature named [Ghor Ghor Mo Mo]. This creature appears when a strong thunderbolt occurs in the evening. It would hear the sound of grumpy kids who were nagging and would come to take them. After taking the kids to a dark, distant place, Ghor Ghor Mo Mo would leave them in the darkness so that no one could hear their voices anymore.‌ Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Ghor-Ghor-Momo** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Ghor-Ghor-Momo** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
