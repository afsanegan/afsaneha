Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **DADA KALAAKU** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **DADA KALAAKU** within the mythological taxonomy of *Jin*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour Dada Kalaaku is the name given to a creature that appears like an old woman in the local beliefs of the southern Iranian province of Hormozdgan. Legend has it that this creature could emerge from underwater with a hooked stick when people are fetching water from wells. It is believed that the chances of this creature appearing are especially higher when a person goes to a well or water storage alone. The hooked stick is said to be used to catch the legs of the visitors so that it can pull them in the water.Today this legend is used in the south of Iran to discourage children from visiting wells and water storages for the fear that they might drown. … Oral history, Local Lore"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **DADA KALAAKU** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **DADA KALAAKU** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
