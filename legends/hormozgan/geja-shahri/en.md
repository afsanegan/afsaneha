Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Geja-Shahri ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Geja-Shahri ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami 'Gaje Shahri', is the name of a Jin present in the folklore of the people of Markazi Province, Iran. 'Gaje Shahri' means 'Guardian of the Night'. This Jin is very tall and thin and looks like a spirit.Gaje Shahri enjoys scaring those who like to work at night or put an all-nighter; people like farmers who work on their farms or stock breeders who take care of their livestock at night.Sometimes Gaje Shahri follows these people to their houses and scares them. It also says 'Gach todom bil'e' using a scary tone, which means 'run or I'm gonna catch you". Gaje Shahri doesn't hurt anybody. It only enjoys scaring those who stay up late. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam None"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Geja-Shahri ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Geja-Shahri ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
