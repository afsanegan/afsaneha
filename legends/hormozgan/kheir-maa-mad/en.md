Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Kheir Maa-Mad** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Kheir Maa-Mad** within the mythological taxonomy of *Jin*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour here's a myth about a creature named 'Kheir-āmad'. Kheir-āmad is a very filthy humanoid Jinn that eats trash. At night, this creature comes to people's doors and knocks. If they ask 'who is it' before opening the door, it won't answer. If they open the door without asking 'who's it', Kheir-āmad will either kill them or eat them. That is why they told children to always ask before opening the door at night and that if there was no answer, not to open the door since Kheir-āmad is behind it. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Kheir Maa-Mad** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Kheir Maa-Mad** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
