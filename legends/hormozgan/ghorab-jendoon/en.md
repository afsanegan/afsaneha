Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Ghorab Jendoon ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Ghorab Jendoon ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz Ghorab Jendoon or Ghorab Jenni is the "ghost ship" which people in Bandar Abbas, especially the Hormozgan and Minabi sailors, belived in. They would describe this ship as very huge and fully decorated with string ligths all over. Sailors say when someone would see this boat, it would give a sudden and strong feeling of it moving towards them with an extreme speed making them believe it wants to drown their boat, but it will never reach them resulting the sailors to throw themselevs into the water! If someone wanted to go towards Ghorab Jendoon, it would create an illusion that its so close but no matter how far they would go with whatever speed, they would never reach it; Ghorab Jendoon would just disappear in a beat. Translation: Nasim Rasoulipour …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Ghorab Jendoon ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Ghorab Jendoon ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
