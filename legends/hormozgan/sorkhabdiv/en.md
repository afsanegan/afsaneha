Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Sorkhab Div ‌ ‌‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Sorkhab Div ‌ ‌‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *Div*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz In the epic of Borzu-nama, there's a Div named [Sorkhab]. He also has a brother named [Puladvand]. They are named in the context of a war with the hero of the epic, Borzu. When Pouladvand tells his troops that Borzu is planning to invade China and kill him, His brother Sorkhab suggests that they would write a letter to trick Borzu. His plan is hence, "Write a letter to Borzu and tell him that if he accepts the truce and changes his mind about attaching, Pouladvand will give him both China and his daughter's hand in marriage." Borzu receives the letter and, after reading it, furiously rips it apart. Seeing his disrespectful act, Sorkhab attacks him and gets slapped by Borzu. He falls unconscious. Later, when he wants to continue the fight, Borzu grabs him by his horn, beheads him, and then kills his child, Ghertās. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Sorkhab Div ‌ ‌‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Sorkhab Div ‌ ‌‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
