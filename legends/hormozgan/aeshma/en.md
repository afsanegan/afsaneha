Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Aeshma** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Aeshma** within the mythological taxonomy of *Div*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour According to the belief of ancient Iranian, there's a div named 'Aeshma'. Aeshma is the demon of anger and fury. Whenever this Div fails to bring evil to 'good creations', it tries to cause chaos and conflict between other divs and 'bad creations'.Aeshma is fed by those with mal-intent who choose anger and the anger of those who drink alcoholic liquor.'Soroush' (the angel of conscience), who is the symbol of obedience and religious purity, prevents Aeshma's wrongdoings and saves the world from anger and its destructive effects. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Aeshma** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Aeshma** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
