Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **Ichacheh** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Ichacheh** within the mythological taxonomy of *Jin*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami According to the people living in the villages in Natanz city (located in Isfahan. central Iran), there's a creature known as [Ichecheh]. There are two tales about this creature:Tale one:Ichecheh is a short Jin wearing a hat. This Jin is interested in yellow objects like gold or yellow candies. It's also harmless. They say that one day Ichecheh went to an old woman's house to eat Nabat (Persian yellow rock candy). The old woman takes Ichecheh's hat. It starts following and begging the old woman to give it back. Ichecheh says, "Give my hat back, and I will give you anything you want". The old woman asked for some Ashrafi (old currency) and gave Ichecheh's hat back.Tale Two:Ichecheh is a short Jin similar to Bakhtak with a hat on its head. At night it sits on people and causes them trouble breathing and moving. They say that one night Ichecheh sleeps on top of an old woman. The old woman takes Ichecheh's hat away, and Ichecheh runs away. When the old woman looks at the hat in her hands, she sees lots of gold and coins attached to it, and she quickly hides them in a flour sack. Ichecheh went to the old woman's every night to get its hat back. One day when the old woman gets home, she sees that the sack is empty and the hat is gone. They say that Ichecheh ran from the window and disappeared into thin air. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Ichacheh** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Ichacheh** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
