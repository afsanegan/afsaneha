Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Hormozgan** (encircling the historical domains of **قشم / بندرعباس**), the legendary lore of **DORANJ** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **DORANJ** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami In the belief of the people of southern Iran, there is a Jinn (Genie) named "Doranj" or "Drouj" who has a long-standing enmity with humans. He lives in deserts or remote places, and it feeds on the flesh of animals, children, and innocent people. This Jinn has been described in two ways: one is a tall, swarthy old woman with a terrible figure, and the other is a man with a long, and disheveled beard, thick eyebrows, and a broad nose. Doranjs are scary creatures who can shapeshift. Some believe that these creatures are humanoid animals that live in the mountains, and could occasionally capture young people and enslave them. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **DORANJ** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Hormozgan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **DORANJ** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
