Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Razavi Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **نیشابور / توس**), the legendary lore of **GEL BINI** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **GEL BINI** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour In the belief of Kashmiri people in the Khorasan Province, there is a creature called "Gelbini", literally meaning, mud nose. Gelbinis are invisible creatures that go along rivers and streams in the middle of the night and seek mud, to make noses for themselves. After doing so, for the purpose of being exact, they come to the village and seek a sleeping human to compare their nose to theirs. Once they find a human they like the shape of the nose of, they lay on them putting their nose against theirs, creating an opposite version of the human's nose. It is believed that if the person wakes up at this time, they will suffer from sleep paralysis. It is said that if they could overcome the paralysis and grab the creature's nose, Gelbini would ask them a question. If the person answers, he will show him a treasure, and if not, he will kill that person and take him away. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **GEL BINI** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Razavi Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **GEL BINI** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
