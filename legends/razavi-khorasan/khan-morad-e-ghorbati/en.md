Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Razavi Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **نیشابور / توس**), the legendary lore of **Khan Morad-e-Ghorbati ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Khan Morad-e-Ghorbati ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour In the past, when there was no sewage system, people used pits and poured the water they used for washing clothes or dishes into the alleys. Sometimes they accidentally poured the used water on the passersby. Whenever that happened, the passersby replied 'Check who's passing before pouring the water. If you pour it on Khan-Morad-Ghorbati by accident, he'll get angry and leave a mark on your house and the people in the house will never be in peace again'. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam None"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Khan Morad-e-Ghorbati ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Razavi Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Khan Morad-e-Ghorbati ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
