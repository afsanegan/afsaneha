Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Razavi Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **نیشابور / توس**), the legendary lore of **Hadhayush** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Hadhayush** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami Also known as “Sarsaok”, the giant primordial cow is one of the mythical bovines from the medieval creation text, the Bundaheshn. Soon after the advent of humanity, the first people mounted Hadhayosh to access the three “eternal fires” on its back. They traversed the earth astride the being as if it were a giant ship able to transport large populations. It is believed that this cow still lives, and in the final battle between Ahura Mazda (the supreme creator god) and Ahriman (chief of all evil), Hadhayosh will be slain by Soshiant (the final Saviour). From Hadhayosh’s essence, a substance for eternal life and immortality will arise containing the cow’s fat mixed with the white Haoma (a sacred mythical plant). "Gubad Shah," who is half-human and half-ox, protects this cow until the final battle between good and evil. … https://www.mythicalcreaturesguide.com/hadhayosh/"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Hadhayush** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Razavi Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Hadhayush** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
