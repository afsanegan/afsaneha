Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Yazd** (encircling the historical domains of **یزد / میبد**), the legendary lore of **GobadShah** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **GobadShah** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour Gubad Shah/GŌBADŠĀH, is the name of a immortal mythical ruler seen in old Iranian religious texts. According to the Bundahišn (Medieval Iranian Creation Text) GubadShah is the son of Agreerah and Pashangaan. It is said that he was the “Spiritual Master” of lands neighbouring Iran, perhaps somehwere between Turkistan(Lands north east of Iran, Transoxiana) and Chinestan(China). He is described as “ox from foot to waist and man from the waist up.” He sits constantly on the shore of a lake, worshipping the gods and pouring libations into the lake, so killing innumerable animals that are harmful or repulsive. He is said to be the keeper of Hadayānš, whom’s fat provides elixir of immortality at the end times."

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **GobadShah** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Yazd to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **GobadShah** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
