Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Mazandaran** (encircling the historical domains of **آمل / دماوند**), the legendary lore of **Bom-e-Mar** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Bom-e-Mar** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz According to the people of Mazandaran (northern Iran), Bom-e-Mar, or the Snake On The Roof, is the master of all snakes. This snake, which is probably a venomous viper, lives on the roof of old houses. Some say it's the guardian of oppressed, sacred places and buried treasures. It lives a long life. Some even say this snake could live in certain homes for seven generations. Fathers tell their sons, on their will, not to hurt this snake and to leave a bowl of water on the roof on warmer days. According to some tales, in order to protect the house they live in, this snake can knock down the intruders using only one bite. It is believed that this snake does not seek to hurt people who do not have mal-intent. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Bom-e-Mar** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Mazandaran to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Bom-e-Mar** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
