Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Gilan** (encircling the historical domains of **رشت / لاهیجان**), the legendary lore of **Kekh Bu ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Kekh Bu ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour Kekh-boo', in the beliefs of the people of Gilan (northern Iran), is the name of a bird. It is also known as the perception of the sound of a bird. It's told in tales that once, a newlywed was milking the cows under her cranky father-in-law's supervision. A little while after she finished milking the cows, she took the milk to her father-in-law. He claimed that the milk in the bucket was less than the amount she had milked earlier and had taken some of it for herself. Trying to defend herself, she said 'Kekh boo' or 'Kaf bood', meaning it was foams and bubbles which made the milk look more in the beginning. Her father-in-law didn't accept what she was saying and still accused her of stealing. Heartbroken, she asked God to turn her into a bird so that she can fly from that house. God granted her wish and turned her into a bird. As a bird, the newlywed kept shouting 'kekh boo', 'kekh boo'. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Kekh Bu ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Gilan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Kekh Bu ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
