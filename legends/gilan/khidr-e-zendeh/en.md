Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Gilan** (encircling the historical domains of **رشت / لاهیجان**), the legendary lore of **Khidr-e-Zendeh** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Khidr-e-Zendeh** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz Khidr-e-Zendeh, according to the Kurdish people of northwestern Iran, is a man who turned into an angel. They say that he used to be an irresponsible man. One night, after getting drunk, he got out of the bar and started walking in the alleys. He noticed a paper on the ground with the word 'God' written on it. He picked it up and tried to find a better place to put the piece of paper on. After failing to find a better place, he swallowed the piece of paper. Seeing what the man did, God forgave all his sins and turned him into a guardian angel on earth. Khidr-e-Zendeh attends many places and helps orphans or people in danger of genies. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Khidr-e-Zendeh** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Gilan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Khidr-e-Zendeh** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
