Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Gilan** (encircling the historical domains of **رشت / لاهیجان**), the legendary lore of **Pappoi** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Pappoi** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Sadaf Kazemian According to the people of Gilan province (northern Iran), 'Pappoi' is the name of a bird that sits every night in spring and summer and cries all night long. Pappoi's singing voice sounds like grieving or a request for sympathy.Pappoi used to be the name of a boy who had lost his mother. It is not clear whether his mother was dead or lost. Pappoi's father then married another woman. The woman kept abusing and hurting the boy. One day, the woman poured boiling water on Pappoi's hands. Out of pain, he longs for his mother and wishes to turn into a bird. One night his wish comes true and he turns into a bird. Since then, he always sits somewhere and sings and talks to his mother which is why his singing voice sounds like a cry. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Pappoi** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Gilan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Pappoi** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
