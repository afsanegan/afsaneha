Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Golestan** (encircling the historical domains of **گرگان**), the legendary lore of **Akh-Vakh** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Akh-Vakh** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Yalda Rasekhi ‏According to the people of Ramian (a city in Golestan province located in the northern part of Iran), there's an enormous creature named 'Akh Vay'.‏Akh Vay is not visible to the eyes and people can only hear it's voice. Whenever it makes noise, It's as if the creature is saying 'Akh Vay'. If anyone hears this creature's voice, it possesses them or enters their body and causes illness.‏It is also said that this creature was how they used to picture bacterias. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam None"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Akh-Vakh** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Golestan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Akh-Vakh** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
