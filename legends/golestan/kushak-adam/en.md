Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Golestan** (encircling the historical domains of **گرگان**), the legendary lore of **KEWŠAK ĀDĀM** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **KEWŠAK ĀDĀM** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mo.Rasoulipour According to the local lores of the people of Ramian (town in northern Iran), there used to be creatures called Kewsak Adam. These creatures used to live in the forests of Ramian, such as Nilbarg, or Zarbin cypress reserve.Kewsak Adams were a type of forest dwarf. They were short but thin and had similar features to the early human; features such as having a body full of hair.Green blood flew through their veins, and they were immortal. They also feared humans and tend to watch them from afar.Translated by Sara Mohebbi … Oral history, Local Lore"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **KEWŠAK ĀDĀM** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Golestan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **KEWŠAK ĀDĀM** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
