Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Golestan** (encircling the historical domains of **گرگان**), the legendary lore of **Sakineh** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Sakineh** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami Sakineh is the name of a creature in the beliefs of the people from Koohsar village located in Golestan province (northern Iran). According to the tales, she came during the night and took children away with her.According to Tat people (a group of people living in northern Iran) in Golestan (Northern Iran), There's another story about Sakineh. It said that Sakineh was a woman who wandered around in the woods like a ghost. If people captured her, she brought good luck; if not, she brought disasters. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Sakineh** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Golestan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Sakineh** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
