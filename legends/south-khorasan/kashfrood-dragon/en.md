Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **South Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **بیرجند / طبس**), the legendary lore of **Kashfrood Dragon ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Kashfrood Dragon ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *AŽDAHĀ (Dragon)*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz KashfRood is the name given to a giant Dragon in Shahnameh. The tale states that this dragon was laying across Kashfrood river in Khurasan and due to its size and mass, had blocked the flow of water, resulting in drowght and death of cattle. The locals were unable to deal with the danger of expelling the dragon. Sawm, Iranian hero and the grandfather of Rostam, later alleges to the King of Iran that it was he who took up the challenge and defeated the dragon. Translation: Nasim Rasoulipour …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Kashfrood Dragon ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of South Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Kashfrood Dragon ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
