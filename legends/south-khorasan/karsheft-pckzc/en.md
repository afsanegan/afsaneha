Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **South Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **بیرجند / طبس**), the legendary lore of **KARSHEFT** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **KARSHEFT** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Mohammad Rasoulipour Kershft is the name of a mighty mythical bird in Iranian mythology. Kersheft is the biggest and leader of meat-eating birds. The word Kersheft means "Quick-flight", which might point to the speed at which the bird maneuvered the world. In Budaheshn (Iranian Cosmology and Creation Story), it attests that Kershft is not only able to speak but that it brought religion to Yima (Jameshed's) legendary tower and rampart in which Jamshid built this building to protect a selection of creatures from the severe snow and cold that happened in his era. Due to this, the language of the religion was spoken in the bird's tongue. According to this story, Kersfat, the counterpart of Simorgh, is the master of all birds. In the book of Vendidad, Farfard II, Kershift is mentioned with the name Kershipter, and its appearance is more or less like an eagle. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **KARSHEFT** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of South Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **KARSHEFT** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
