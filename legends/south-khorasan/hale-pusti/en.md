Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **South Khorasan** (encircling the historical domains of **بیرجند / طبس**), the legendary lore of **Hale Pusti** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Hale Pusti** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz [Hale Pusti], according to the beliefs of the people of southern Khorasan, is the name of a creature with terrifying skin. It goes to nosy and rude kids whenever this song is sung: "Hale Pusti, Come and stretch your mouth open, Take *the child's name* away and free us of the burden." Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Hale Pusti** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of South Khorasan to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Hale Pusti** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
