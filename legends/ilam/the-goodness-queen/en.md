Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Ilam** (encircling the historical domains of **دره‌شهر**), the legendary lore of **The Goodness Queen ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **The Goodness Queen ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz 'Malake-ye-Kheir' or 'the goodness queen', according to the beliefs of people from 'Jaber' village (Kurdish clans of Ilam in western Iran), is a creature among the race of Jinns which has the role of helper.The Goodness Queen sometimes heals the people of the village. She also sends help to those in need or those who are lost. Some say that they have seen her. They described her as a tall woman with long black hair who dresses in a long black, white and pink dress. They also say that she doesn't walk and floats over the ground.She doesn't want to scare people, and if she senses that she's caused fear, she disappears immediately. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **The Goodness Queen ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Ilam to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **The Goodness Queen ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
