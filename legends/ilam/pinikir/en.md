Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Ilam** (encircling the historical domains of **دره‌شهر**), the legendary lore of **Pinikir** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Pinikir** within the mythological taxonomy of *Deity*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Yalda Rasekhi A clay tablet obtained from excavations in Susa leads us to 37 Elamite gods. At the top of this list appears the name of Pinikir, or Pinkir or Pingir as the mother-goddess.There are pieces of evidence that show Pinikir was known as an Elamite warrior goddess. She had many followers, especially in Susa.While she is attested in Elamite documents infrequently, she achieved a degree of prominence in Hurrian religion. Due to her presence in pantheons in many parts of the ancient near east (from Anatolia to Iran), modern researchers refer to her as the 'cosmopolitan deity'.The Elamites eventually considered Pinikir to be the god of the skies and to have the power of curses. This respect can indicate the culture of matriarchy in ancient Elam. Translation: Zeinab Hashemi"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Pinikir** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Ilam to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Pinikir** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
