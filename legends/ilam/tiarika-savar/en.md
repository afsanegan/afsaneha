Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Ilam** (encircling the historical domains of **دره‌شهر**), the legendary lore of **Tiarika Savar ‌** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Tiarika Savar ‌** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz Tiarika Savars, in Elam's mythology, are the knights of darkness who sometimes attack Jinns and other supernatural beings. They can be seen at night under the moonlight. Some say that they are men riding horses with shadow-like appearances. Tiarika Savars make themselves look like wolves whenever they encounter humans because they do not want to be recognized. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Tiarika Savar ‌** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Ilam to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Tiarika Savar ‌** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
