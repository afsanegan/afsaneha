Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Tehran** (encircling the historical domains of **ری / تهران**), the legendary lore of **KANAS DIV** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **KANAS DIV** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami Is the name given to a demon that eats human flesh in 11th-century Persian epic, Faramarz-nama. It is written that Kanaas had stolen three daughters of the king of India. The Indian king then sends a messenger to ask the Persian King, Kavoos to help kill the demon and bring back his daughters. in this midst, a Persian hero with the name of Faramarz volunteers and sets to see the deed done. After a long dangerous journey where his mental and physical abilities are tested, he overcomes Kanaas and frees the princesses. He also finds that the demon had stored much booty in his cave that now he could claim for himself. …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **KANAS DIV** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Tehran to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **KANAS DIV** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
