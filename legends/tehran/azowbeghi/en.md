Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Tehran** (encircling the historical domains of **ری / تهران**), the legendary lore of **Azowbeghi** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Azowbeghi** within the mythological taxonomy of *Jin*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Farima Keshavarz Azowbeghi is a mythical demon known in the Southern Khorasan area of Iran. It is believed that it appears to people in a talking sheep/goat body and transform to a human body once they reach them. These creatures are not evil but are the reason many shepards or people alone in the woods get scared at night sometimes, are Azowbeghis. They live in their own communities and have their own families. Translator: Nasim Rasoulipour …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Azowbeghi** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Tehran to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Azowbeghi** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
