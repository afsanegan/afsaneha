Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Tehran** (encircling the historical domains of **ری / تهران**), the legendary lore of **DUAL PA** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **DUAL PA** within the mythological taxonomy of *موجود افسانه‌ای*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Dual Pa|Daval Pa Illustration by: Erfan Aghvami In Iranian beliefs, there is a creature called Doval Pa. He looks like an old man or a weak man with a human-like upper body with limp long loose legs some say up to forty yards long. Doval means a strap or a long strap, and Doval Pa means a creature that has legs like a strap. He appears in the way of the passers-by in the form of a weak and helpless old man, and by lamenting and begging, he asks for help. He asks them to help him and take him home. Once, fooled a person into carrying him, the old man grows out his strap-like legs and tightly wraps around the helper's waist. If the victim tries to put him down, he puts pressure on his back and side so much that the person would suffocate. The only way to get rid of him is to get him drunk so that the passer-by can get rid of him. (ashwood.ir)"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **DUAL PA** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Tehran to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **DUAL PA** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
