Preserved across centuries within the rich oral folklore of **Tehran** (encircling the historical domains of **ری / تهران**), the legendary lore of **Chashmak Div** stands as a compelling testament to Iran's enduring mythic imagination. Transmitted across hearths, mountain paths, and rural caravans, the tale has survived through the living memories of local elders.

Folklorists generally categorize **Chashmak Div** within the mythological taxonomy of *Div*. As with many traditional entities of Iranian folk belief, it embodies both the untamed natural elements and the ancestral anxieties regarding solitary wilderness. Classical collectors recorded its traditional traits as follows:

> "Illustration by Erfan Aghvami ‏‏In the belief of ancient Iranian people, [Cheshmak-Div] (meaning the winking demon) was the name of a demon that could cause tornadoes, earthquakes, and destruction. People believed that this demon was in constant conflict with clouds and rain. It is told in Bundahishn that Cheshmak Div can cause earthquakes with blinking one eye and tornadoes with the other. The role of this demon in Zoroastrian fables is to destroy cities and villages and to break down the trees. Translation: Sarah Mohebbi Moghadam … …"

In local oral tradition, an encounter with **Chashmak Div** was regarded as a formidable threshold between familiar domestic reality and the unseen realm. Elders customarily advised night travelers, shepherds, and caravan drivers traversing the historic passes of Tehran to heed ancient apotropaic rituals—such as carrying iron amulets, maintaining solemn reverence towards nature, and never uttering provocative words—to pass through its territorial domain unharmed.

Today, the chronicle of **Chashmak Div** forms an invaluable component of Iran's intangible cultural heritage, offering modern readers an evocative window into how ancient communities interpreted their ecology, mystery, and moral balance.
